# Stepper: decisions

**Status:** published directly, without a Lab step (2026-10-16, built from the roadmap note "Progress through the steps of a task"). Category Effects, as on the roadmap. The page opens on a four-step checkout (Cart, Shipping, Payment, Review) with Back and Next; the one example is Vertical, with descriptions. The index card shows four indicators without titles (`preview.card`).

- **What it is:** where someone is in a task with a fixed order of steps. shadcn has no stepper, so the parts are ours, named the shadcn way (and like the common community stepper): `Stepper`, `StepperItem`, `StepperTrigger`, `StepperIndicator`, `StepperTitle`, `StepperDescription`, `StepperSeparator`.
- **API:**
  - `Stepper`: `value` / `defaultValue` (1) / `onValueChange`, `orientation` (`horizontal | vertical`). The value is the current step, from 1; the steps before it are completed, and a value past the last step marks them all done (a finished checkout).
  - `StepperItem`: `step` (required), `completed` (overrides the rule either way), `disabled` (its trigger can't be pressed: a step not reachable yet).
  - `StepperTrigger`: a `button` (or a link with `asChild`) that goes to its step. A disabled link gets `aria-disabled` and doesn't navigate.
  - `StepperSeparator` can be written in every item: it hides itself in the last one.
- **Semantics:** an `ol` (named with `aria-label`), so screen readers say how many steps there are; each step is an `li`. Each trigger is in the tab order, named by its title (the indicator is `aria-hidden`); the current one has `aria-current="step"`. Enter and Space press it, as any button. There's no WAI-ARIA pattern for steppers, so the meta has no pattern link, and no roving focus: steps are ordinary buttons, as in a breadcrumb.
- **Layout:** the trigger is a grid with implicit columns: the indicator spans two rows, the title sits beside it and the description under the title, so users write the parts flat, without wrappers (Card's CardAction uses the same trick). The title is `leading-6`, the indicator's height, so the two centre on one line. Horizontal: each item is the trigger then the line (`flex-1`), and the line sits on the indicators' middle (`mt-3 -translate-y-1/2`). Vertical: the line runs down the indicators' middle (`start-3`), 4px clear of each circle, and each item but the last has `pb-6` for it.
- **Look:** neutral. Indicator: a 24px circle, `bg-muted` with a muted number; current and completed are `bg-primary`. The current step has a 1px ring 3px outside the circle (`border-primary/30`), and completed steps show the check. Title muted until the progress reaches it, then `foreground`; hover brightens it. The line is 2px, `bg-border` with a `bg-primary` fill. Flat, no shadows.
- **Motion (the crafted part): progress travels along the line.**
  - **Moving on,** the step you leave swaps its number for a check: the number lifts away through the blur (`duration.fast`) as the check draws itself in (`pathLength`, Checkbox's check and timing). The line to the next step fills (`scaleX`, or `scaleY` vertical, from the start edge; a CSS transition, `duration.slow`, `ease-in-out`: an eased tween, never a spring, whose overshoot would show progress that isn't there). As the fill arrives, the next step takes the ring (it grows from 0.8 as it fades in, `duration.base`), its fill fades to primary and its title brightens.
  - **Jumping several steps** fills each line in turn, 0.35s apart (one line's duration), and each step on the way gets its check as the fill reaches it, so it reads as one continuous progress rather than everything changing at once.
  - **Going back plays it backwards:** the ring leaves at once, the lines empty towards the step you picked (from their far end), and as the emptying reaches each step, its check erases (`duration.fast`, `ease.in`) and its number comes back through the blur.
  - **How the timing works:** the Stepper remembers the step the change came from. Each item works out its delay from its distance to it (the indicator, title and trigger use one delay, the line another), as inline `transition-delay` and motion `delay`. Once the progress has arrived, the remembered step catches up and every delay is 0 again, so hovering a step never waits.
  - **Fills and colours only fade** (motion.md: a blurred solid shape reads as a glow); only the number, which is content, comes and goes through the blur. Nothing moves on first paint (`initial={false}`, and CSS transitions don't run on mount). With reduced motion every delay and duration is 0, and the number's hidden state stays the same, so the server's HTML always hydrates.
- **Demos:** the checkout disables the steps ahead (they aren't reachable yet) and lets you press any step you've reached; Next past Review marks every step done. When Back or Next can't go further, focus moves to the other one, so it's never lost. Vertical lets every step be pressed, to show the lines filling and emptying in turn.
- **Tests:** a named list of four steps, buttons named by their titles, `aria-current` on the current one, the states; filled lines after completed steps, aria-hidden, the last one hidden; pressing a step; the delays of a three-step jump (lines and titles); none with reduced motion; Tab, Enter and Space; a disabled step can't be pressed; controlled, and past the last step; vertical; axe.
- **Not built yet:** a loading or error state per step; content panels tied to the steps (use the value to show your own); titles under the indicators in the horizontal layout; a smaller or larger size; linear mode built in (disable the steps ahead yourself, as the demo does).
